A CRM organizes your sales process: who each customer is, which stage they’re in and what’s missing to close. The real question isn’t whether your company needs one, but **which kind of CRM** fits: an off-the-shelf one paid per user every month, or a [custom one](/en/services/custom-crm) that you own.

## What an off-the-shelf CRM is

It’s a ready-made product you subscribe to. It has plenty of features, sets up quickly and the vendor handles updates. In exchange, you pay a license for every user, adapt the way you sell to the system and your data lives on someone else’s platform.

## What a custom CRM is

It’s a system built from scratch for your operation. Pipeline stages, record fields and reports are the ones your team actually uses. There are no per-user licenses and, when the project ends, **you own the code and all access**.

## Quick comparison

| | Off-the-shelf CRM | Custom CRM |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Monthly license per user | Project investment + hosting |
| Setup | Fast, but you adapt to it | In stages, shaped to your process |
| Team adoption | Often drops with unused screens | High: only what your team uses |
| Integrations | Whatever the vendor offers | Whatever your operation needs |
| Ownership | The vendor’s | Yours |

## When an off-the-shelf CRM makes sense

- Your sales team is small and your process is standard.
- You need to start this week and don’t yet know how you sell.
- You have no special integrations with other systems.

## When a custom CRM makes sense

- Your sales process has its own steps: quotes with rules, approvals or multiple currencies.
- You need to connect the CRM to WhatsApp, your website or your inventory system.
- Per-user costs grow every time you add a sales rep.
- You want the data and the system to belong to your company.

## 5 questions to decide

1. How many people will use the CRM two years from now?
2. Which steps of your sale don’t fit a standard pipeline?
3. Which tools does the CRM need to talk to?
4. How much will you pay in licenses over three years?
5. What happens to your data if you switch vendors?

If two or more answers worry you, a custom CRM is probably the better investment.
