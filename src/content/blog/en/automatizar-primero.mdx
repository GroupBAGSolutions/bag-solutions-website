Automation isn’t about putting robots everywhere. It’s about making repetitive tasks run on their own so your team can spend time on what actually needs judgment. The challenge is choosing **what to automate first**.

## A simple method: three questions

For every manual task in your company, ask:

1. **How often does it happen?** Daily or weekly adds up far more than monthly.
2. **Does it follow clear rules?** If the decision is always made the same way, it can be automated.
3. **How much does an error cost?** Miscopied data in invoices or orders gets expensive.

Tasks with high frequency, clear rules and costly errors are your first candidates.

## Examples that usually pay off fast

| Process | What gets automated | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier invoices | Data extraction and posting to accounting | No retyping, no copy errors |
| Expense approvals | Rules by amount and approval from your phone | Answers in minutes, not days |
| Customer updates | WhatsApp or email messages by order status | Fewer “where’s my order?” calls |
| Weekly reports | Data that consolidates itself | The report is ready every Monday |

## What not to automate yet

- Processes that change every week or aren’t defined yet.
- Decisions that rely on an expert’s judgment.
- Tasks that happen once a year.

First organize the process; then automate it.

## What about the systems you already use

Automation connects tools that offer an **open API**. We don’t integrate software with restricted access or proprietary licenses, such as A2, Saint or SAP. If your process depends on one of them, we evaluate alternatives during the assessment.

## How to measure the savings

Before starting, estimate how many hours a week the process takes and how many errors it produces. That baseline lets you measure the result with clear numbers.
