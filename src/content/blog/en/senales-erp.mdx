An ERP connects every area of the business in one system: when you sell, stock goes down; when it runs low, a purchase order is created; and finance sees it instantly. These are the clearest signs it’s time.

## 1. Spreadsheets don’t match each other

Sales keeps one file, the warehouse another and accounting a third. Every month-end starts with the same question: which number is right?

## 2. Nobody knows the real stock

You sell products you don’t have or overbuy “just in case.” Inventory is counted by hand and there’s always a gap.

## 3. Data is typed two or three times

The order is written in a chat, then in a spreadsheet and then in the invoice. Every time data is copied, an error appears.

## 4. Month-end close takes days

Gathering sales, collections and payments to see how the business did takes several people and a lot of patience.

## 5. Decisions come too late

You find out a product isn’t moving or a customer isn’t paying when it’s already a problem.

## 6. Growing means hiring for admin work

Every new branch or product line adds hours of manual work instead of sales.

## 7. Information depends on one person

If whoever manages the master spreadsheet goes on vacation, operations stall.

## How to start without stopping operations

An ERP doesn’t have to be rolled out all at once. The best approach is **in stages**:

1. **Assessment:** find the area that causes the most time loss and errors.
2. **First module:** usually inventory or sales, with your company’s real data.
3. **Integration:** add purchasing, invoicing and finance on the same foundation.
4. **Reports:** metrics to decide with up-to-date data.
